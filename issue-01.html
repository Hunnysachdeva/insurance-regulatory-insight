INSURANCE REGULATORY INSIGHT — ISSUE 01

Customer Interest Before the Sale

September 2026 · IRDAI sends a strong message on policyholder protection

₹1 Crore Penalty. One Customer. A Much Bigger Industry Lesson.

The insurance industry is entering a phase where regulatory compliance, customer suitability and responsible distribution are becoming inseparable from business growth.

In a recent order, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) imposed a ₹1 crore penalty on Canara HSBC Life Insurance in connection with lapses concerning policyholder interests. The action followed concerns around the sale of a deferred annuity product to an 88-year-old customer, along with issues relating to suitability, verification and documentation.

The regulator's action sends a message that every insurance professional should pay attention to:

A successful sale is not simply a policy issued. It is a policy sold appropriately, transparently and with the customer's informed interest at the centre.

What does this signal for the insurance industry?

The regulatory direction is becoming increasingly clear. Insurance companies, brokers, agents, corporate agents and other distribution partners need to look beyond sales volumes and premium acquisition and focus on the quality of business being generated. Five areas deserve immediate attention:

1. Customer suitability

Does the product genuinely match the customer's age, needs, financial objectives and risk profile?

2. Need-based selling

Product recommendations should be driven by customer requirements rather than merely by product availability or sales targets.

3. Informed consent

Customers must understand what they are purchasing, including key benefits, limitations, obligations and financial implications.

4. Documentation & verification

Proposal forms, benefit illustrations, verification calls and supporting documentation are not merely procedural requirements. They form an important part of the customer's protection.

5. Distributor accountability

The responsibility for customer experience increasingly extends across the entire distribution ecosystem.

What changes for sales & distribution leaders?

For sales leaders, this is an important shift in mindset. Traditionally, performance has often been measured through revenue, premium, policies and targets. The emerging model is broader — revenue plus persistency, customer suitability, compliance and experience.

That means a high-performing sales team of the future will not simply be the team that sells the most. It will be the team that generates quality, sustainable and compliant business.

What about general & commercial insurance?

The principle is equally relevant to general and commercial insurance. For fleet owners, SMEs and corporate customers, a responsible insurance conversation should begin with understanding:

  • Business activity
  • Vehicle/fleet profile
  • Risk exposure
  • Existing coverage
  • Claims experience
  • Coverage gaps
  • Appropriate policy structure
  • Customer expectations

This creates an opportunity for brokers and insurance professionals to move from transactional selling to consultative risk advisory. And that is where long-term relationships are built.

My take

The biggest lesson from the latest regulatory action is not simply about a penalty. It is about where the insurance industry is heading.

The regulator's increasing focus on policyholder protection means that sales, compliance, operations and customer experience can no longer operate in silos.

A sales professional who understands only targets may generate business. A professional who understands customer needs, product suitability, compliance and service can build a sustainable portfolio. The future belongs to the second category.

The new insurance equation

This is more than a compliance framework. It is a business-growth strategy.

Long-Term Trust

What should insurance leaders do now?

1. Review sales practices

Identify areas where aggressive target-driven selling could create customer or compliance risks.

2. Strengthen frontline training

Equip teams to explain products clearly rather than simply pitch them.

3. Improve documentation discipline

Treat proposal forms, verification and customer communication as critical controls.

4. Monitor quality of business

Track persistency, complaints, cancellations and customer feedback alongside premium numbers.

5. Build a compliance-first culture

Compliance should be embedded into the sales process — not introduced after the sale.

Final thought

The Indian insurance industry has tremendous growth potential. But sustainable growth will not be measured only by how much premium the industry generates. It will increasingly be measured by how much trust it creates.

Insurance is ultimately a promise. And every policy sold is an opportunity to either strengthen — or weaken — that promise.
